## 1. Background & Organizational Structure

The Arts Fest duty list was originally organized under my supervision. Every student assigned a duty was briefed multiple times, with strict, clear wording, so they understood the responsibility they were taking on. Briefing and properly updating students is essential — only once volunteers genuinely understand the importance of their job will they perform it seriously, rather than "just for the sake of doing something."

For the **district-level Arts Fest (Kalolsavam)**, the structure was:

- **Teachers** were in charge of preparing the duty list.
- **Heads** were in charge of corrections and helping teachers finalize the list, and ensuring the overall event ran smoothly.
- **My specific assignment**: working with **Sajini Miss (S-A-J-E-E-N-E)** on Guruthipuram students' participation in the district-level Kalolsavam — not the hosting logistics, but everything related to our own students' participation, particularly the **on-stage items**.
- **Off-stage items** were convened separately, in a meeting last Saturday.
- I was also part of the **decoration team**, working with **Imima**, which is why I arrived early on event day.
- The Sajini Miss duty was assigned to me at the last moment, after she requested extra hands. I committed to being present from morning until the end of the event, and brought in additional reliable people to help — because although the existing team was genuine, more hands were needed.

## 2. Incident: Morning Refreshment Shortage

During the morning, while working downstairs, I jokingly suggested we get water since we'd been talking ("yapping") a lot and our throats were getting dry. This casual comment led to uncovering a real problem:

- **Letha Miss** went to the refreshment room to request refreshments and was told there was **no list** of volunteers/participants to reference.
- I followed up with **Renu Ma'am**, who was in charge of food. She confirmed there was no list to work from.
- By the time I brought her a list (after morning snacks had already been served), she informed me the food had **run out**.
- Root cause identified: the **helpdesk downstairs had been given food first**, with no proper calculation of numbers, and some children had taken more than one serving. Renu Ma'am acknowledged this but noted it's hard to penalize children directly for taking extra.

### Points to Note:
- We need **built-in flexibility** in snack/refreshment quantities to account for extra volunteers who join beyond the original headcount.
- The **exact number of volunteers must be shared with the refreshment team in advance**, and cross-checked with the helpdesk allocation so food isn't front-loaded to one location.

## 3. Volunteer List Problems

Before the event itself, there was a deeper issue with how the volunteer list was compiled:

- Several students who worked diligently and put in significant effort — including myself, at one point — were **left off the official list**.
- I raised this, and other teachers also raised similar concerns, but these requests were **not taken seriously**.
- The likely reason: organizers default to a comfortable, familiar set of students rather than expanding the pool.
- This is not sustainable — for a much larger event, we cannot rely on the assumption that "a particular set of students work and others don't." That assumption itself needs to be tested and revised. I already raised these list issues directly with the students concerned, the day before the event.
- Underlying issue, to come back to: this is fundamentally a **supervision problem** — if the concern is that unfamiliar students might not perform well, the answer is better supervision of new volunteers, not exclusion of them from the list.

### Points to Note:
- We need to be **more diligent about incorporating suggestions** that come from multiple sources (students, teachers) rather than defaulting to a fixed circle.
- List-making should have a mechanism to **cross-check for overlaps** — I had already prepared a document (received from Divya) for checking duty overlaps, which I shared with Renu Ma'am for this reason, along with an explicit note that I had no involvement in compiling that particular list myself.

## 4. Helpdesk Staffing (Downstairs vs. Upstairs)

There was a clear **staffing imbalance**:

- **Downstairs helpdesk**: overstaffed in the morning — reports suggest 10–15 people were stationed there, well beyond what was needed after the initial morning rush.
- **Upstairs/top-floor helpdesk**: understaffed — **Fabian Sir** reported "running around like crazy" alone on the top floor, while most volunteers had gravitated downstairs.
- Teachers confirmed: there is a genuine rush at the downstairs helpdesk **only in the morning period**; after that, the need drops significantly. It doesn't need to be removed, but the number should scale down.
- Teachers were also seen running around guiding parents themselves because there simply weren't enough dedicated people doing that job — parents themselves told me they needed more people helping them.

### Points to Note:
- **Do not remove the downstairs helpdesk** — it's necessary given the size of the campus and the need to guide students/parents, who find a large, unfamiliar campus overwhelming.
- **Do not simply send excess volunteers away** — instead, **redistribute** them to other areas of campus that are short-staffed (e.g., guiding parents to specific locations, wayfinding support, upstairs support).
- The core principle: **right-size** the helpdesk to time-of-day needs, and **reallocate**, rather than either overstaffing or cutting.

## 5. Guest & Parent Management

Feedback from teachers was clear: we should not simply "point fingers" toward a direction when guests or parents ask for help — we should **actively guide them**.

### Points to Note:
- We need a dedicated **"Welcoming Team"** — stronger than what existed this time — focused specifically on making parents and guests feel like the campus is genuinely welcoming, not just directionally informed.
- We need a separate **crowd/guest management** function, distinct from the helpdesk, to handle the flow of people arriving, especially around judge arrivals.
- General principle: **the fewer times we simply point in a direction instead of walking someone there, the better.** This doesn't mean escorting every single guest is practical, but wherever a guest looks genuinely lost or unsure, we should proactively help rather than gesture and move on.
- Overall tone should be warm and personal, almost like the welcoming, caring manner associated with figures like Adidev Baba — guests should feel like this is their own school, not an unfamiliar institution they're passing through.

## 6. Judges: Escort & Respect Protocol

This was a specific pain point:

- Judges explicitly **prefer to be escorted by teachers**, not students, and expect this as a mark of respect and proper welcome.
- On the day, we ended up using **students** to escort judges to classrooms because there simply weren't enough available teachers — everyone was already occupied.
- Only **Helen Miss** had the master list of judge assignments, which caused people to run around without clear direction.
- I personally took the list from Helen Miss and was told we should personally escort judges to their classrooms.
- The current arrangement: judges are escorted to the stage in the morning, after which **volunteers take over responsibility for judges** once they've reached their assigned stage/room.

### Points to Note:
- For the **morning arrival window specifically**, we need enough **teachers** (not just students) assigned to escort judges, per their stated preference.
- The **judge list must be distributed to more than one person** in advance — relying on a single point of contact (Helen Miss) created unnecessary confusion.
- This same escort standard — proper, respectful, systemized — should also apply to **on-stage events**, not just the morning arrival.
- Underlying principle: **judges (and by extension, all guests) should be treated with the highest level of respect and care**, not simply pointed toward a room number.

## 8. Section-Level Volunteer Supervision Structure

Given the distances between rooms/blocks (e.g., GBS is a separate, large block), a more structured supervision model is needed:

### Proposed Structure:
- **One (or two) trusted, diligent volunteers per major section/block** (e.g., one or two dedicated volunteers just for GBS).
- This volunteer's role: ensure everyone else in that section is doing their duty responsibly — essentially a **section lead**, suggested and vetted by teachers.
- **One additional person (teacher-designated)** should specifically check in on this section lead periodically to confirm they, too, are performing diligently.
- The section lead should be in regular contact with the concerned teachers to confirm organization is holding up.
- **If any volunteer is underperforming, they should be swapped out immediately**, with immediate notice — this should be treated as a strict, non-negotiable standard.
- Teachers should feel **free and encouraged to report issues** with specific volunteers — not just complain generally, but actively flag underperformance so the responsible section lead/coordinator can swap them out or correct the issue on the spot.

## 9. Volunteer Badge Policy

Several issues arose around how volunteer badges were handled:

- Volunteers wearing badges were seen **wandering during breaks** (e.g., lunch) without returning them.
- Common teacher consensus (though I personally have some reservations about how strict this needs to be) is that **badges should be returned when a volunteer goes on break**, especially longer ones, and reissued clearly once duty resumes.
- We must instill an understanding that **wearing the badge = accepting a responsibility**, not just a decorative pass.
- **Meal coupons**: last year (e.g., during the basketball tournament), meal coupons were properly distributed to teachers. This year, that process was **not followed through properly**, and it needs to be corrected going forward.

### Points to Note:
- Badges must **not be shared** between students under any circumstances — if found doing so, or misusing the badge, this should be treated seriously.
- Volunteers should wear the badge only while on active duty; when taking a break, the badge should be handed back until they return.
- There should **always be at least one active volunteer holding the post** at any given time — if one person needs rest, they should be swapped, not simply leave the post empty.

## 10. Break & Rotation Discipline

A specific incident highlighted a gap: a volunteer given a short break (~10–15 minutes) by a teacher ended up being gone for **over two and a half hours**.

### Points to Note:
- Breaks should be **short and bounded** (10–15 minutes is reasonable), not open-ended.
- **One person must always be present at each post** — a rotation system (swap-in/swap-out) should be used rather than leaving a position unmanned.
- This ties directly back to the badge policy: badge returned during break, reissued/reclaimed upon return to duty.

## 11. Seating Arrangements for Volunteers & Teachers (Off-Stage Areas)

This is a structural/logistics gap identified by comparing our event to prior benchmarks:

- **Reference point 1**: At **Sarga Sangamam**, last year's district-level event, benches and desks were provided outside each stage, with teachers stationed there to help organize.
- **Reference point 2**: At the district-level Kalolsavam previously hosted at **Placid Vidya Vihar**, each stage had a **bench and desk set up outside**, with a clear structure: **two teachers and one volunteer inside**, and **one volunteer outside**, per stage, at all times.
- At our event, volunteers stationed outside stages had **no seating** at all — they were left standing for extended periods.
- Consequence: volunteers standing around with no fixed "post" naturally start chatting, drifting, or losing focus — not because they are bad volunteers, but because there's no structural anchor keeping them in place. This isn't a character issue with the students; it needs proper teacher supervision, but also a physical fix.

### Proposed Structure:
- Provide a **bench/desk outside each stage** — giving volunteers a literal **default position** to return to, rather than aimlessly standing around for extended stretches.
- Split responsibilities: **registration/attendance-type tasks** (e.g., "chess numbers," check-ins) can be handled **outside**; **evaluation/timing-related tasks** can be handled **inside**.
- With **10 stages total**, and additional teachers coming in from state-level participation, it should be feasible to assign **at least one teacher per stage outside**, mirroring the Placid Vidya Vihar model.

## 12. Training Gap: 11th Grade Boys

There was criticism that the 11th-grade boys underperformed in their duties. Important context and reframing:

- These students are relatively new to this kind of responsibility and, in some cases, come from partner/other schools with less direct exposure to how our council operates.
- The core issue is **not attitude or capability** — it's that they were **not properly briefed** on the weight and importance of their specific role.
- Critical point for training: **this is a district-level event that we are hosting.** Unlike an internal school-level mistake, which stays contained within our own campus, a mistake here reflects on the school in front of the entire district — participating schools, judges, and parents from outside.

### Points to Note:
- The **student council and teachers together** need to run a clearer briefing process that explicitly communicates the **stakes and visibility** of a district-level hosted event — not just "here's your task," but "here's why this task matters and what happens if it's dropped."
- This briefing gap is likely a root cause behind several of the issues above (list confusion, badge misuse, helpdesk overcrowding) — volunteers who don't understand the "why" behind a rule are less likely to follow it under pressure.

## 13. Duty Continuity Across Event Days (Day 1 → Day 2)

A separate scheduling principle came up regarding how we assign duty across the two days of the event:

- Some students have their own competition items on Day 1 and are completely free on Day 2 — or vice versa.
- The instinct might be to say "this student had duty/items on one day, so don't bother giving them duty on the other day" — **that instinct is wrong.**
- If a student is free on Day 2, they should be actively considered for duty on Day 2, regardless of what they were doing on Day 1. Availability should not go to waste just because it's a "second day" assignment.
- At the same time, there's a continuity consideration: students who worked Day 1 will have already learned the flow of the event, the layout, and how things run — that institutional knowledge is valuable and shouldn't be thrown away.

### Proposed Structure:
- **Core/"upstream" team members** (those in leadership or coordination-heavy roles, who need to understand how the whole event runs) should **remain constant across both days** wherever possible, since they've already built up context from Day 1.
- **"Downstream" / supporting roles** can be more flexible — these are good positions to bring in students who were occupied with their own items on Day 1 but are free on Day 2.
- This lets us use total available manpower more efficiently across both days, without losing the continuity that keeps the event running smoothly.

### Points to Note:
- Do not exclude a student from Day 2 duty simply because they had events or duty on Day 1 (or vice versa) — check their actual Day 2 availability.
- Keep core coordination roles consistent across both days for continuity; use newly-available students to fill supporting roles on the second day.
